Easy DIY Halloween Home Decor Ideas

Turn your home into a cozy Halloween wonderland with these easy DIY decor ideas.

The Halloween season is finally here, and if you're anything like us, you've probably already started thinking about pumpkins, cozy candles, and all things spooky.

The best part about decorating for Halloween? You don't need to spend a fortune to make your home feel festive. A few simple DIY projects can completely transform a corner of your living room, front porch, dining table, or entryway.

Whether you love a classic spooky look, a cozy autumn-inspired style, or something a little more playful, these DIY Halloween home decor ideas are easy to recreate and fun to make. Most of them use items you may already have at home, making them perfect for a last-minute decorating session.

GHOSTLY GREETINGS

A friendly ghost decoration is one of the easiest ways to add Halloween charm to your home. Simple fabric, old bedsheets, or lightweight white cloth can instantly become adorable floating ghosts.

Place them near the front door, hang them from tree branches, or style them in a corner with pumpkins and lanterns. They create a spooky atmosphere without looking too scary, making them perfect for family-friendly Halloween decorating.

PUMPKIN DISPLAY PERFECTION

Nothing says Halloween quite like pumpkins. Instead of placing a single pumpkin on the porch, try grouping different sizes together for a more layered look.

Mix real pumpkins, painted pumpkins, and mini gourds to create visual interest. Add lanterns, candles, or autumn leaves around them for a display that feels warm, welcoming, and festive.

WITCH'S CORNER MAGIC

Transform a small corner of your home into a playful witch-inspired display. A broomstick, a few old books, black candles, and a decorative hat can instantly create the look.

Layer different heights and textures to make the arrangement feel more interesting. It's a simple way to bring Halloween personality into your space without overwhelming the room.

SPOOKY CANDLE GLOW

Candles instantly make a space feel cozy, especially during autumn evenings. For a Halloween twist, use battery-operated candles and decorate the holders with faux spider webs, black ribbon, or painted details.

Arrange them on shelves, coffee tables, or mantels. The soft glow creates a magical atmosphere that feels festive while still remaining elegant.

HAUNTED MANTEL MOMENTS

Your mantel is one of the easiest places to create a Halloween focal point. Start with a garland, layer in pumpkins, and add a few decorative Halloween accents.

Mix different materials such as wood, fabric, glass, and metal to create depth. A well-styled mantel can instantly make the entire room feel ready for the season.

FLOATING HAT DISPLAY

One of the most popular DIY Halloween decorations is a floating witch hat display. Suspend lightweight hats using clear string to create the illusion that they are floating in the air.

This idea works beautifully above dining tables, entryways, or outdoor patios. It looks impressive but is surprisingly easy to recreate at home.

PUMPKIN VASE ARRANGEMENT

Give your pumpkins a fresh twist by turning them into decorative vases. Hollow out a pumpkin and fill it with autumn flowers, dried stems, or seasonal greenery.

The combination of natural textures creates a beautiful seasonal centrepiece that works throughout the entire autumn season, not just Halloween.

SPOOKY TABLE SETTING

A few simple details can completely transform your dining table. Layer dark napkins, pumpkins, candles, and seasonal foliage to create a Halloween-inspired tablescape.

You don't need elaborate decorations. Sometimes a handful of carefully chosen elements creates a more elegant and memorable look than an overly decorated table.

DIY HALLOWEEN WREATH

A Halloween wreath is a simple way to make your front door feel festive. Start with a basic wreath form and add seasonal elements such as mini pumpkins, ribbon, leaves, or decorative Halloween accents.

The best part is that you can customize it to match your personal style, whether you prefer something spooky, whimsical, or neutral.

COZY SPOOKY SHELVES

Shelves are often overlooked during seasonal decorating, but they offer plenty of opportunities to add Halloween charm. A few small pumpkins, candles, decorative books, and festive accents can completely change the look of a shelf.

Keep the styling balanced and avoid overcrowding. A few thoughtfully placed decorations often have a much bigger impact than filling every available space.

Halloween Indoor Decor Ideas: 20 Cozy, Spooky & Creative DIY's

Discover easy DIY Halloween decor ideas to make your home cozy, creative, and spooky.

HOME DECOR

04 SEPTEMBER, 2026

Disclaimer: All products featured on OKAD are independently selected by our editors. However, we may earn a small commission when you purchase products through links on our site, at no additional cost to you.

Halloween decor can be so much more interesting than covering every surface with orange plastic pumpkins and fake cobwebs. The most memorable spaces often use a few clever ideas to completely change the mood of a room.

Think warm light glowing through tiny windows, paper bats flying across a wall, a giant moon above a reading corner, old books becoming part of a haunted library, or a simple doorway turning into a scene from a mysterious story.

And the best part? You don't need expensive decorations or complicated crafting skills to create these looks.

This collection focuses on DIY Halloween decorations that are actually possible to make at home. For every idea, you'll find what you need, how to make it, how to style it, and little tricks that help the finished result look intentional rather than homemade in the wrong way.

So choose your favorite idea, gather a few things you already have around the house, and let's make your home feel a little more magical, mysterious, cozy, and spooky this Halloween.

1. Make a Giant Moonlit Wall

If you want one Halloween decoration that immediately changes an entire room, start with a giant moon.

How To Make It

Instead of buying a large Halloween wall decoration, make your own oversized moon from lightweight cardboard or thick paper. A large shipping box can work perfectly for the base. Draw a large circle, cut it out, and cover it with layers of crumpled paper to create a slightly uneven surface.

How To Style It

Don't center everything perfectly. Put the moon slightly to one side and allow the bats to travel diagonally across the wall. That small change makes the arrangement feel more like a real nighttime scene.

You Need

Lightweight cardboard, paper, scissors, adhesive, and a warm flameless light.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Living rooms, bedrooms, reading corners, and small spaces.

2. Create a Shadow Theater On Your Wall

Turn a blank wall into a mysterious Halloween scene using nothing more than paper silhouettes and warm lighting. It is simple to make, but the finished effect can completely change the mood of a room.

How To Make It

Cut simple shapes from black paper or lightweight cardstock, such as crooked trees, bats, cats, birds, rooftops, branches, or a tiny haunted house. Arrange the larger shapes toward the center or lower part of the wall and use smaller shapes farther away to create the feeling of distance.

How To Style It

Don't arrange the silhouettes in a straight line. Let the branches curve upward and allow a group of bats to move diagonally across the wall. Leave some empty space between the shapes so the scene feels atmospheric instead of crowded.

You Need

Black paper or cardstock, scissors, removable adhesive, and a warm flameless light.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Blank walls, hallways, bedrooms, living rooms, and apartments.

3. Turn Your Window Into A Haunted Night Scene

Your window can become one of the most beautiful Halloween decorations in your home because it already works like a natural picture frame. A few simple silhouettes can make it look like a mysterious scene from an old house.

How To Make It

Cut black paper into simple shapes such as bare branches, bats, birds, or the outline of a distant house. Place the larger shapes around the corners of the window and let a few smaller details move inward toward the center.

How To Style It

Keep the middle of the window mostly open so the design doesn't feel heavy. Add a sheer curtain behind the silhouettes and place a warm flameless light nearby to make the scene especially atmospheric in the evening.

You Need

Black paper, scissors, removable adhesive, a sheer curtain, and an optional warm flameless light.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Bedrooms, living rooms, apartments, and entryways.

4. Build A Tiny Haunted Village

A miniature haunted village gives you a chance to create an entire Halloween world in one small area. It looks detailed and impressive, but the basic pieces can be made from cardboard or cardstock.

How To Make It

Create several simple house shapes from lightweight cardboard, small boxes, or cardstock. Make each one slightly different in height and shape, then add tiny doors, windows, rooftops, fences, trees, or paper pumpkins.

How To Style It

Place the tallest house toward the back and the smaller houses in front. Add crumpled brown paper or a piece of neutral fabric underneath to create a simple landscape. Put small battery-operated lights behind the houses so the windows appear to glow.

You Need

Cardboard or cardstock, paper, scissors, adhesive, small decorations, and battery-operated lights.

Difficulty

Moderate

Best For

Shelves, consoles, coffee tables, side tables, and small spaces.

5. Create An Enchanted Forest Corner

Instead of decorating an entire room, transform one quiet corner into a tiny enchanted forest. This idea brings together natural textures, soft lighting, and subtle Halloween details for a look that feels magical rather than crowded.

How To Make It

Place a few sturdy natural branches inside a container and arrange them so they reach upward and outward. Add paper leaves, small pumpkins, woodland-inspired decorations, and a few warm flameless lights around the base.

How To Style It

Give the corner a little story by adding one unexpected detail, such as a tiny paper doorway, miniature mushrooms, a small sign, or a moon behind the branches. Keep the colors simple with cream, charcoal, brown, muted orange, and warm light.

You Need

Branches, paper, adhesive, small decorations, a sturdy container, and warm flameless lights.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Empty corners, entryways, reading areas, and bedrooms.

6. Create A Floating Witch Hat Display

Give an ordinary ceiling or empty corner a little Halloween magic with witch hats that appear to float in midair. This is one of those simple decorations that creates a surprisingly dramatic effect.

How To Make It

Use lightweight paper or fabric witch hats in several sizes. Arrange them at different heights and slightly different angles so they don't look like they are hanging in a perfectly organized row.

How To Style It

Concentrate the hats above one area, such as a dining table, reading corner, or entryway. Let a few hats sit higher and farther apart while keeping others closer together. Add warm lighting below them so they cast soft shadows on the surrounding wall or ceiling.

You Need

Lightweight witch hats or paper hats, suitable hanging material, and optional warm flameless lighting.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Dining areas, entryways, reading corners, and small spaces.

7. Turn A Shelf Into A Haunted Library

Give your everyday bookshelf a mysterious Halloween personality without replacing everything on it. A few changes in arrangement and some small handmade details can make an ordinary shelf feel like an old haunted library.

How To Make It

Rearrange your books by mixing vertical rows with horizontal stacks. Wrap a few books in plain brown or dark paper if you want a more vintage appearance. Add small pumpkins, dried branches, paper bats, old-looking frames, and tiny objects between the books.

How To Style It

Don't place every decoration along the front edge of the shelf. Push some pieces farther back and bring a few forward to create depth. Place taller branches behind shorter objects and tuck a warm flameless light behind the books for soft shadows.

You Need

Books, paper, small pumpkins, branches, frames, and optional warm flameless lighting.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Bookshelves, wall shelves, home offices, and living rooms.

8. Create A Moody Pumpkin Corner

Instead of placing one pumpkin on every surface, create one beautiful pumpkin arrangement that becomes a focal point. Mixing different shapes, sizes, and finishes makes the display feel collected and intentional.

How To Make It

Choose pumpkins and gourds in different sizes and combine natural pumpkins with a few painted ones in cream, charcoal, brown, or muted orange. Start with the largest pumpkin toward the back, then layer smaller ones in front and slightly to the side.

How To Style It

Add dried leaves or branches around the base, then place the arrangement on a tray, stool, bench, or low basket. Leave some empty space around the display so the pumpkins become the focus instead of making the area feel crowded.

You Need

Pumpkins or gourds, dried foliage, a tray or small surface, and optional warm flameless lighting.

Difficulty

Very Easy

Best For

Living rooms, entryways, bedrooms, and apartments.

9. Turn A Mirror Into A Moonlit Scene

Give an ordinary mirror a magical Halloween transformation by turning it into part of a moonlit nighttime scene. The idea is subtle enough to keep the mirror useful while still making it feel completely different.

How To Make It

Cut a large moon, tiny stars, bats, and branch silhouettes from paper. Place most of the decorations around one edge of the mirror instead of covering the entire surface.

How To Style It

Let the branches appear to grow around the frame, with a few bats moving upward toward the moon. Keep the reflective area mostly clear and place a warm flameless light nearby to create gentle shadows across the surrounding wall.

You Need

Black and cream paper, scissors, removable adhesive, and an optional warm flameless light.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Hallways, bedrooms, entryways, and bathrooms.

10. Make A Dark Floral Halloween Arrangement

Halloween flowers don't have to be bright orange or covered in obvious spooky decorations. A moody arrangement of dried foliage can bring the season into your home while still feeling elegant and natural.

How To Make It

Gather dried branches, grasses, leaves, and dried flowers in earthy or darker tones. Place them in a ceramic vase, old pitcher, or simple container. Start with the tallest branches and gradually add shorter stems around them.

How To Style It

Allow a few branches to extend naturally beyond the main arrangement so it feels slightly wild and imperfect. Add one or two tiny paper bats or a small pumpkin beside the vase rather than filling the flowers with Halloween decorations.

You Need

Dried foliage, branches, a vase or container, and a few small Halloween accents.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Dining tables, consoles, mantels, bedrooms, and entryways.

11. Make A Haunted Doorway

Give an ordinary doorway a dramatic Halloween transformation without covering the entrance in bulky decorations. A few simple silhouettes and natural textures can make it feel like the entrance to a mysterious old house.

How To Make It

Cut long branch shapes from black or brown paper and arrange them around both sides of the doorway. Let some branches curve inward as though they are growing around the entrance. Add a few paper bats and a small moon or vintage-style sign above the doorway.

How To Style It

Keep the center of the doorway completely clear so it remains easy to use. Concentrate the details around the outer edges and let a few shapes extend upward to create height. Use a limited palette of charcoal, brown, cream, and muted orange to keep the display sophisticated.

You Need

Black or brown paper, scissors, removable adhesive, dried foliage, and a few lightweight Halloween decorations.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Entryways, hallways, apartment doors, and living room entrances.

12. Create A Cozy Halloween Storybook Nook

Turn a small reading corner into a scene that feels like it belongs inside a mysterious Halloween story. You don't need a lot of decorations—just a few carefully chosen pieces that work together.

How To Make It

Start with a chair and a small side table. Add a few old-looking books, a pumpkin, dried branches, a dark throw blanket, and a warm flameless light. Then choose one storytelling detail, such as a paper moon, mysterious artwork, or tiny haunted house.

How To Style It

Keep the chair as the main focus and arrange the smaller details around it. Place the tallest element behind the chair or beside the table, then layer smaller objects toward the front. Leave some open space so the corner still feels cozy rather than crowded.

You Need

An existing chair, books, a small table, fabric, one or two Halloween accents, and a warm flameless light.

Difficulty

Very Easy

Best For

Bedrooms, living rooms, reading corners, and unused spaces.

13. Make A Vintage Halloween Sign

A handmade Halloween sign can become a beautiful focal point when it looks slightly aged and imperfect. Instead of buying a mass-produced sign, create one that feels like it has been part of your home for years.

How To Make It

Use a piece of lightweight cardboard or a simple board as your base. Cover it with muted cream, brown, charcoal, or another soft vintage-inspired color. Add a short phrase such as "Enter If You Dare," "Beware The Night," or "Haunted House" using simple, classic lettering.

How To Style It

Place the sign against a wall, above a doorway, or inside a Halloween display. Surround it with dried branches, a few pumpkins, old books, or a warm flameless light. Don't make the arrangement too perfect—the handmade character is part of its charm.

You Need

Lightweight cardboard or a simple board, paper, adhesive, drawing or lettering supplies, and optional Halloween decorations.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Entryways, shelves, mantels, hallways, and gallery walls.

14. Style A Halloween Gallery Wall

Give your everyday artwork a temporary Halloween personality without taking everything off the wall. A few mysterious images can completely change the feeling of an existing gallery arrangement.

How To Make It

Add inexpensive or handmade prints featuring moons, botanical drawings, ravens, mysterious landscapes, silhouettes, or vintage-inspired Halloween artwork. Mix these with artwork you already have instead of replacing every picture.

How To Style It

Use the same frame color or similar paper tones throughout the arrangement so the different images feel connected. Keep the darker Halloween pieces slightly separated instead of grouping them all together. This makes the wall feel like a collected art display rather than a themed decoration.

You Need

Existing picture frames, printed or handmade artwork, paper, and removable adhesive if needed.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Living rooms, hallways, bedrooms, home offices, and entryways.

15. Create A Ceiling Of Flying Bats

Most Halloween decorating happens at eye level, which makes the ceiling a surprisingly effective place to create a dramatic display. A flock of paper bats can make an ordinary room feel completely different.

How To Make It

Cut bats from black paper in several different sizes. Make some larger and more noticeable while keeping others small enough to look farther away. You can fold the wings slightly to give some of the bats a more dimensional appearance.

How To Style It

Start with a small group near one wall and gradually spread the bats outward and upward. Create a gentle curve rather than a straight line so the group looks like it is moving through the room. You don't need hundreds of bats; a carefully planned arrangement can have a much stronger effect.

You Need

Black paper, scissors, and suitable removable adhesive or lightweight hanging material.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Dining areas, hallways, bedrooms, living rooms, and small spaces.

16. Give Your Coffee Table A Halloween Makeover

Your coffee table can become a small Halloween scene without losing its everyday function. The key is to create one intentional arrangement instead of covering the entire surface.

How To Make It

Start with a tray or a small stack of books. Add one medium-sized pumpkin, a small vase filled with dried branches, and one or two unusual decorative objects. Finish with a warm flameless light if you have enough space.

How To Style It

Try using three different heights: something tall, something low, and something in between. For example, use a branch arrangement as the tall element, a pumpkin as the low element, and a book or small object between them. Leave enough open space for the table to remain practical.

You Need

A tray or books, a pumpkin, a vase, dried foliage, one or two small decorations, and optional warm flameless lighting.

Difficulty

Very Easy

Best For

Living rooms, apartments, small spaces, and family rooms.

17. Make A Simple Halloween Mantel

A Halloween mantel can become the focal point of your living room without needing a huge collection of decorations. The secret is to choose one strong centerpiece and build around it.

How To Make It

Choose a large frame, mirror, paper moon, or vintage-style Halloween sign as your main piece. Add dried branches, pumpkins, paper bats, and warm flameless lights around it.

How To Style It

Create height near the center and gradually bring the decorations down toward both sides. Don't make the two sides perfectly identical. Slight asymmetry gives the arrangement a more natural, collected appearance.

You Need

One focal piece, pumpkins, dried branches, paper decorations, and warm flameless lights.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Fireplaces, mantels, consoles, shelves, and dressers.

18. Create A Mini Potion Display

Turn a small unused shelf into an apothecary-inspired Halloween display using simple decorative containers and natural details. It is a fun way to add personality without taking up much space.

How To Make It

Collect small empty jars, decorative bottles, dried herbs, leaves, paper labels, tiny pumpkins, and old-looking books. Create playful labels such as "Moon Dust," "Midnight Garden," "Autumn Mist," or "Forest Leaves."

How To Style It

Place the tallest bottles toward the back and shorter jars toward the front. Use a few books underneath some of the containers to create different levels. Add a warm flameless light behind the arrangement so the display has a soft glow.

You Need

Empty decorative containers, paper, labels, books, dried natural materials, and optional warm flameless lighting.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Shelves, desks, consoles, bathroom counters, and small corners.

19. Create A Cozy Halloween Dining Table

You don't need a completely themed table to make Halloween feel special. A few natural textures, soft lighting, and small seasonal details can create a warm and inviting dining space.

How To Make It

Start with a neutral or dark table runner. Add pumpkins, dried foliage, and a few warm flameless lights down the center. For each place setting, add a tiny paper bat, miniature pumpkin, autumn leaf, or handmade name card.

How To Style It

Keep the centerpiece low enough that people can easily see across the table. Instead of creating one large arrangement, use several small clusters with space between them. This makes the table feel layered while keeping it practical for everyday use.

You Need

A table runner or fabric, pumpkins, dried foliage, paper, small decorative details, and warm flameless lights.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Dining rooms, breakfast areas, and small dinner gatherings.

20. Build One Complete Halloween Story

For the final idea, don't think about decorating a single surface. Think about creating a complete little scene. Choose one corner of your home and give it its own Halloween story.

How To Make It

Start with one large element, add one medium element, and then finish with several tiny details. For example, you could use a giant paper moon as the large element, a branch arrangement as the medium element, and paper bats, leaves, tiny pumpkins, and warm lights as the smaller details.

How To Style It

Choose a simple story before you begin. It could be a mysterious reading corner, a tiny haunted house, an enchanted garden, or a moonlit forest. Make sure every object contributes to that story instead of adding decorations simply because you have them.

You Need

One large focal piece, one medium decorative element, several small Halloween details, and warm flameless lighting.

Difficulty

Easy

Best For

Living rooms, bedrooms, entryways, reading corners, and any unused area you want to transform.
